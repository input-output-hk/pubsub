The workstream

What we're working towards

An innovation workstream: starting from research proposals and product-driven use cases, we design and prototype a Cardano-anchored publish/subscribe layer — identifiable publishers, verifiable events, on-chain topic commitments, and Sybil resistance from main-chain stake.

  • Node builders → node operators — authenticated emergency alerts from teams like IOG, Amaru, and the Cardano Foundation.
  • SPOs → delegators — announcements from topics that provably belong to the pool.
  • dReps → delegators — voting intent and rationale (CIP-1694).
  • dApps → users — critical alerts and dApp-specific, per-user notifications (e.g. open positions) under on-chain identities.
Trusted communication for Cardano
Before
  • Critical messages travel over X, Discord, Telegram — entirely off-chain.
  • Anyone can impersonate an SPO or a governance actor.
  • No canonical channels — audiences can't know where legitimate messages appear.
  • No delivery guarantee — a message may simply never reach its audience.
  • Spam and Sybil accounts cost the attacker nothing.
After
  • Publishers identified by on-chain credentials.
  • Every message cryptographically verifiable.
  • Topics committed on-chain — subscribers know what they subscribed to.
  • Delivery to every honest subscriber — formally analysed, and guaranteed while attackers stay below a known share of the network.
  • Spam and fake identities carry a real on-chain cost.

The guarantees Cardano gives transactions, extended to communication.

Phase 1 — exploratory research

From proposal to evidence

  • Where we started: the four use cases just shown, and the research paper SecureCyclon: Dependable Peer Sampling (July 2023).
  • The proposal: three layers — peer sampling, navigation, dissemination — security inherited across the layers.
  • What we did: tested those claims formally — simulation and probabilistic model checking.
  • What we found: pull from upstream peers you pick — with push, a node may simply never be picked.
  • Uniformity fails, and silent attacks bias a node's view — none of the nine defences fire.
  • Where that led us: a simpler, modellable design — no peer sampling yet; the node registry shuts out silent attacks.

Analysed what we were given, widened to alternatives like Basalt — then narrowed to a modular base built to grow.

Evidence-based architecture
Before
  • Security rests on layered gossip with sampled, private views.
  • Properties assumed to be inherited between layers — not verifiable in operation.
  • Attack cost is constant: a handful of nodes suffices at any network size.
After
  • Network membership anchored on-chain — public, authenticated, verifiable.
  • Delivery paths seeded by verifiable on-chain randomness.
  • Attack cost grows with the network — security improves as Cardano grows.

The unverifiable private state that broke peer sampling is removed by design.

Phase 2 — the method

Earning the design, not proposing it

  • Five candidate topologies (M1–M5), from the simplest — push to a few random peers — up to hybrid designs, all facing the same adversary: a fraction μ of silent Byzantine nodes.
  • One bar to clear: a good graph — every message from every honest publisher reaches every other honest subscriber of the topic.
  • Two independent tracks: formal analysis — per-model laws for coverage, bandwidth, hops, and degrees — cross-validated against Monte-Carlo simulation and prototype experiments.
  • Measured trade-off: good-graph probability (security) vs hops (latency) vs messages (bandwidth) — per configuration, not in the abstract.

If the final design carries complexity, it is because the data shows the simpler variants fail — and exactly why.

Phase 2 — where we are

Progress

  • All five models specified as executable formal models with per-property analyses — laws self-tested against their own simulators and cross-validated against independent algorithms.
  • M3 is the current favourite on intuition — standing links plus per-epoch pull — but intuition decides nothing here: the verdict waits on the experiment data.
  • Working Rust prototype node — topics, subscriptions, publishing, fan-out forwarding, and on-chain-style registries.
  • Deterministic simulation framework — controlled, reproducible experiments on protocol behaviour.
Direction

Next steps

  • Complete the formal analysis — validated laws per model, the churn family (degradation, join, repair), and a cross-model recommendation.
  • Run the experiments — the same models measured empirically in the prototype, findings compared against the analytical predictions.
  • Design proposal by mid-August — the measured architecture, its adversary model, positioning against existing art such as GossipSub, and a runnable reference configuration.
  • Remaining weeks of the phase validate and harden it — published as a CIP if time permits.

Outcome: an evidence-backed architecture recommendation Cardano can adopt.

Summary

From unauthenticated announcements to a verifiable, stake-backed communication layer

  • Phase 2 is producing the evidence that will pick the architecture — analysis and experiments together.
