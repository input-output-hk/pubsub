Parameter surface

Fixing the attacker, not its share

Every other chart here fixes the adversarial share and varies something else. That hides the relationship a deployment cares about most, because an attacker does not buy a percentage — it buys identities, and the share they represent rises as the network shrinks. This page fixes the attacker and moves the network instead, deriving the bucket count, admissions budget and pick count that follow.

The three admission parameters are derived here rather than assumed. Each is bound by whichever constraint is tightest at the size you have chosen, and which one that is changes as you move — which is the whole reason the second chart draws all three rather than only the one in force.
