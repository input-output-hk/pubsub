# PubSub - Bi-Weekly Report: 24 July - 6 August 2026

## Executive Summary

This period turned the model family from a design claim into measured evidence. The deterministic experiments framework merged, its cost collapsed by roughly fifty-fold, and the node's selection logic was unified so that all five dissemination models are single-command configurations of one implementation rather than separate code paths. On top of that instrument, **all five models were compared against the formal methods team's closed-form coverage laws and Monte Carlo grids — 21 of 21 coverage cells and all four operating points aligned, with nothing pending** ([PR #138](https://github.com/input-output-hk/pubsub/pull/138), open for review at period close). The analysis side added the two properties the design proposal needs beyond the operating point: how fast security degrades as the adversarial fraction rises, and how bad a failure actually is when it happens — the stranded set is a single node in 61–95% of bad graphs even at strongly elevated μ. Documentation moved from skeleton to draft, with the abstract merged and the remaining specification and rationale sections distributed across tickets. The open design question is unchanged and now the critical path: **the cost of node identity**, where a hybrid proposal combining trust anchors with deposit-priced registration is on the table but not settled, and where slashing remains blocked on the difficulty of proving that a node stayed silent. The phase milestone moved from mid-August to **mid-September** to leave room for the remaining sweeps and the CIP drafting.

## [epic(proto): M-model experimentation](https://github.com/input-output-hk/pubsub/issues/79)

Three merges took the prototype from "one model, measured by hand" to a single instrument that runs the whole family. The **deterministic experiments framework** landed ([PR #102](https://github.com/input-output-hk/pubsub/pull/102)): populations of the crate's real pure core under a round-based wavefront scheduler, seeded honest churn and silent adversaries, graph analytics with miss-cause decomposition and a per-run accounting identity, Wilson-interval statistics, and a byte-reproducible three-artifact output contract where any run replays from its manifest seed alone. Ezequiel's **performance work** then removed the memory wall ([PR #118](https://github.com/input-output-hk/pubsub/pull/118), [ADR 0038](../pubsub-node/docs/decisions/0038-shared-candidate-views.md)) by moving per-topic candidate views behind a shared, self-excluding read seam: an N = 20 000 run peaks at ~0.6 GB where it previously peaked at ~30 GB, which unlocked multicore sweeps — the operating-point sweep dropped from ~15–20 minutes to ~25 seconds and the bulk regime from ~30–40 minutes to ~6 minutes, proven result-neutral by byte-diff against the recorded baselines. Finally the **017 unified selection plane** ([PR #119](https://github.com/input-output-hk/pubsub/pull/119), ADRs 0039 and 0040) collapsed four dial-side strategies and four acceptance baselines into one selection implementation per seam over two fed knobs — bucket count and pick count — which promoted the exact formal M4 from "approximation" to node-expressible with fleet evidence and made all twenty model recipes (M1–M5 across gated and capped variants) knob configurations that fail at startup when misconfigured.

The open [PR #138](https://github.com/input-output-hk/pubsub/pull/138) (ADR 0041) closes the last machinery gap and then uses it: dissemination sends are attributed at emission to their carrying link kind, the sweep configuration gains a publisher sub-table with `forward-to-all` as a legal fan-out kind and k_out as a sweepable axis, and the declared model name is validated against the honest class's wiring per grid point so one configuration name always yields consistent wiring and measurement. M3 is analysed with a seed-aware goodness criterion — per-publisher seed-closure walks on the condensation DAG, matching the formal study's every-publisher check, because a bare one-SCC test would erase exactly the muted-publisher healing M3 exists to provide.

Next: land #138, then run the batch the group agreed on this week — the same models with hash-based connection gating and acceptance caps enabled, which is the first time the experiments stop assuming unbounded honest resources.

## [epic(analysis): dissemination topology models](https://github.com/input-output-hk/pubsub/issues/76)

Two new properties merged across all five models ([PR #116](https://github.com/input-output-hk/pubsub/pull/116), closing [#84](https://github.com/input-output-hk/pubsub/issues/84)). **μ-shift robustness** reads each model's coverage law at a rising effective adversarial fraction with parameters frozen at the μ = 0.2 operating point, giving per-model degradation curves, a budget — the largest μ_eff still meeting δ = 10⁻⁴ — and a collapse point, validated by Monte Carlo at elevated μ. This answers the question the previous period raised but could not yet quantify: not where the threshold sits, but how quickly the guarantee erodes once reality exceeds the assumption. **Failure severity** adds the complementary result: conditional on a bad graph occurring, the stranded set is a *single node* in 61–95% of cases even at strongly elevated μ, across roughly 1 100 observed bad graphs. Together these convert "the probability of a bad graph is 10⁻⁴" into a statement about what a failure costs, which is what a reader of the proposal actually needs. A 22-entry analysis backlog was recorded in `candidate_properties.md`, folding in the churn family and adding security, economics and lifecycle candidates; adversarial flooding is entered and deliberately deferred, on the current assumption that publishers are few and trusted.

The empirical cross-check of the analytical claims also came in during this period via [PR #138](https://github.com/input-output-hk/pubsub/pull/138): every coverage-law value sits inside the Wilson 95% interval and/or within |z| < 2.5 of the formal team's own Monte Carlo counts, across both network sizes and out to the 30 000-run deep tail; cost and latency means at each model's operating point match the published tables to between 0.0004% and 0.024%; and M3's seeding cost measures at exactly s−1 publisher-kind sends per message, making the near-zero-bandwidth healing claim a measurement rather than an argument.

Next: cross-model recommendation ([#85](https://github.com/input-output-hk/pubsub/issues/85)) on the completed evidence, and visualisations comparing the performance metrics across all five models for the proposal.

## [Pub/Sub Phase 2 — empirically-driven architecture](https://github.com/input-output-hk/pubsub/issues/46)

**The milestone moved from mid-August to mid-September** ([PR #130](https://github.com/input-output-hk/pubsub/pull/130)), leaving room for the remaining experiment sweeps and the CIP drafting; the phase still closes 21 September.

Two methodological positions were settled in the weekly session. First, **how to state delivery guarantees**: the bad-graph probability is a deliberately conservative global bound and says only that *some* node goes unserved in an epoch, whereas the per-node probability of non-delivery is substantially lower — and a bad graph does not break delivery for a given publisher unless that publisher is a sink or is surrounded entirely by adversarial peers, which practice further softens because a sender publishes through several peers. The proposal will present both figures rather than only the conservative one. Second, **memory optimisation for the prototype is deferred** in favour of simulation execution speed: a 20 000-node registry view costs roughly 600 MB per node, around 30 KB per entry, which is high but manageable at prototype scale, with two known exits when it matters — hold the registry in a database, or bound the view to a small multiple of RF, which lands in single-digit megabytes and simultaneously restores the honest partial-view sampling interface that the hash-gated selection path currently bypasses.

On the team: Mauro Jaskelioff joins to pick up the analysis contributions as Denis moves most of his time to another workstream, while remaining in the weekly sessions and available for review; code ownership was updated accordingly. Spyros Voulgaris rejoined for a session to review the five models, and confirmed the reading of why M3 leads — it pairs a push mechanism's fast initial spread with a pull mechanism's reliability at the tail, paying for it in structural complexity through its asymmetric use of link kinds.

Next: the hash-gated and capped experiment batch, the five-model visualisations, and the CIP structure.

## [epic(design): solution architecture & CIP](https://github.com/input-output-hk/pubsub/issues/91)

The **abstract merged** ([PR #115](https://github.com/input-output-hk/pubsub/pull/115), closing [#114](https://github.com/input-output-hk/pubsub/issues/114)) and the remaining sections were distributed across tickets: the dissemination protocol ([#131](https://github.com/input-output-hk/pubsub/issues/131)), network identity and address discovery ([#132](https://github.com/input-output-hk/pubsub/issues/132)), topic registration and publisher authorisation ([#133](https://github.com/input-output-hk/pubsub/issues/133)), the rationale and its evidence subsection ([#134](https://github.com/input-output-hk/pubsub/issues/134)), and node registration with identity cost ([#103](https://github.com/input-output-hk/pubsub/issues/103)). The motivation draft ([#105](https://github.com/input-output-hk/pubsub/issues/105)) is complete and lands shortly. Address discovery was explicitly separated from network identity — turning a registered key into a dialable address is entirely outside what the experiments cover and is an open design question in its own right, not a write-up of settled work.

**Node identity is the critical open question.** The proposal on the table binds a fresh per-node key pair to an existing root of trust where the role has one, embedding the anchor into the derivation of the node identifier and requiring valid signatures under both keys; on topics whose anchors are well known — SPOs being the clear case — a valid anchor becomes mandatory for registration and, because it carries reputational weight, can buy a lower deposit, while topics without well-known anchors price the unknown participant higher. This composes vertical identity management where a hierarchy exists with horizontal, deposit-priced identity where it does not; a payment address was ruled out as an anchor, being cheap to generate and carrying no reputation beyond the ADA it holds. Grinding is out of scope for M3–M5, which have no position-based topology to grind toward. The deposit figure itself cannot be fixed ahead of an explicit security budget: calibration runs from the tolerable adversarial fraction to the number of identities an attack needs to the price per identity.

**Slashing remains blocked on evidence, not on mechanism.** A signed-acknowledgement scheme was sketched — downstream nodes return BLS-aggregatable acknowledgements that the upstream accumulates and must partially present at deregistration — and two problems were recorded against it: a downstream node can frame an honest upstream by withholding its acknowledgement, and dropping non-acking peers as a mitigation may not compose with hash-gated link selection. The general obstacle is that when the threat is a node that simply stays silent, local grading functions exist but verifiable proof of misbehaviour does not follow from them. The position for this phase is to specify the economic model and record slashing as later research, rather than make the specification depend on an unsolved proof of a negative. This is consistent with the phase's stated scope, which puts provable-misbehaviour cryptography in phase 3.

Next: refine the identity proposal into the #103 recommendation with its calibration method, and begin the rationale section, which is the furthest along on evidence now that the analysis and the executed comparisons both exist.

Two threads worth recording for later. A **Babel-fee and intent-forwarding use case** over the same protocol was raised and explored, on the reasoning that more live use cases per messaging node makes the case to SPOs for hosting one easier. The objection is structural rather than incidental: a node that receives an intent it could profitably fulfil is motivated not to forward it, so the use case raises adversarial behaviour by construction, and a fulfiller marketplace additionally wants a fairness property the collaborative relay strategies do not provide — nothing in the protocol governs who sees an intent first, which points toward a broker or sequencer. A pooled-reward scheme that pays provably-participating solvers over time, rather than rewarding whoever settles a given intent, would remove the per-intent race and is the one direction worth testing. Recorded as exploration, not a workstream. Separately, the group reaffirmed the **specification-driven approach** for future design work: protocols that exist only as code are hard to review and hard to pick up later, and a specified design lets someone joining late understand not just what was built but why the decisions were made — which is what the ADR and spec trail in this repository is for.
