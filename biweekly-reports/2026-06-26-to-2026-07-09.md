# PubSub - Bi-Weekly Report: 26 June - 9 July 2026

## Executive Summary

This period closed out phase 1 and pivoted the whole effort from *building* the prototype to *experimenting* with it. On the implementation side the peer-view feature landed ([PR #73](https://github.com/input-output-hk/pubsub/pull/73), [issue #75](https://github.com/input-output-hk/pubsub/issues/75)): verifiable hash-gated (bucketed-pull) connection selection and acceptance, an epoch/round split, readiness-gated dialling, and decomposed acceptance baselines — the last strategy seams the experiment program needs. On the research side the team **adopted M3** as the primary network-analysis model (golden nodes removed as seeds, hash-controlled fan-out and seeding, high good-graph probability at 20k nodes) and **formally retired SecureCyclon** as the external position. The two brainstorms fixed the experiment methodology: a pure state-transition simulation framework, a settled metric set (good-graph probability, efficiency, bandwidth), parameter sweeping to find safe/unsafe boundaries, and a three-question per-topic decomposition that keeps the framework model-agnostic. Blink Labs joined the table, anchoring an SPO-notification collaboration via their Raven messenger and the Adder pipeline. The main near-term gate is the experiment harness itself: an N-node spike already reaches 100% coverage at N=200, but sweep/metrics/CSV wrapping and a deterministic core remain before the first real runs.

## [Rust PubSub node prototype](https://github.com/input-output-hk/pubsub/issues/36)

The **005 peer-view** feature merged ([PR #73](https://github.com/input-output-hk/pubsub/pull/73), [issue #75](https://github.com/input-output-hk/pubsub/issues/75), [`005-peer-view`](../pubsub-node/specs/005-peer-view/)), turning the connection layer from "connect to a static set" into verifiable, parameterised strategies. Selection and acceptance are now **hash-gated (bucketed-pull)** — a node deterministically derives its peers from IDs and an epoch nonce ([ADR 0024](../pubsub-node/docs/decisions/0024-verifiable-hash-gated-selection.md)), so spamming connection requests no longer exhausts degree slots. Acceptance became its own seam with an explicit `Rejected` action and no back-fill retry ([ADR 0025](../pubsub-node/docs/decisions/0025-acceptance-seam-and-rejected-action.md)), and the acceptance policy was **decomposed** into `accept-from-all` / `bounded` / `hash-gated` / `hash-gated-bounded` baselines that can be mixed per node. A structural refactor split `Epoch` (the randomness nonce) from the heartbeat dial tick and unified the edge predicate on that nonce ([ADR 0030](../pubsub-node/docs/decisions/0030-heartbeat-interval-and-edge-predicate.md), [ADR 0031](../pubsub-node/docs/decisions/0031-epoch-round-split-and-acceptance-decomposition.md)), while strategy construction moved to per-seam self-construction with role-based upstream/downstream naming ([ADR 0028](../pubsub-node/docs/decisions/0028-strategy-self-construction.md), [ADR 0029](../pubsub-node/docs/decisions/0029-strategies-module-layout.md)). Both inbound requests and heartbeat re-dials are now gated on registry readiness, symmetric to each other.

Next: feed these seams into the experiment framework rather than adding protocol surface — the seams are the configuration knobs (connection/acceptance strategy, `target_degree`, `bucket_count`, `genesis` seed) that the sweeps will drive.

## [Pub/Sub Phase 2 — empirically-driven architecture](https://github.com/input-output-hk/pubsub/issues/46)

Phase 1 wrapped and phase 2 pivoted from building to **experiments**. The agreed vehicle is a **pure state-transition simulation framework** with no async/Tokio dependency: it drives events directly against the node's `NodeState`/`apply` core, giving full control over event ordering, message-delivery history, and data export — enough to analyse *why* a delivery succeeds or fails, not just whether it did. Because strategies are encapsulated closures, switching models (M2 vs M3, directed vs undirected) or adding new ones needs minimal code, and the event-based interface lets new strategies be configured in parallel without conflict. Extensive analysis is supported by running simulations repeatedly and storing raw outcomes to capture the distribution.

The brainstorms fixed the methodology. **Metrics** settled on good-graph probability, efficiency (latency / hop counts), and bandwidth (total messages) — with Denis's point that measurements should target *total* forwarded messages rather than local Request Factor, which can mislead on efficiency. Concrete tracking targets: maximum hop counts, total forwards, and rejected forwards. **Parameter sweeping** (network size, topic counts) was chosen over fixed conservative parameters to find safe/unsafe operating boundaries. **Protocol modelling** was standardised on three per-topic questions — to whom requests are sent, from whom they are accepted, and to whom data is forwarded — keeping the framework model-agnostic so a superior model can be dropped in as a strategy and cross-validated. Scope was trimmed to keep the first framework simple: dynamic periodic re-connection and retry logic are **deferred**; recursive retries on rejection are ruled out (an attack vector), a rejected node accepts the result assuming a low adversarial fraction (mu). For churn, disgraceful (abrupt) leaves count as adversarial while joins and graceful departures are honest; epoch transitions may let two topologies coexist briefly for a seamless handover, at the cost of temporary overhead. An N-node spike ([`experiment-readiness.md`](../pubsub-node/docs/experiment-readiness.md)) already reaches 100% coverage at N = 5/20/50/200 (~0.6 s at 200) on the default strategies.

Next (Ezequiel): finalise the simulation harness with data collection and CSV export by next week, add hop-count and coverage tracking, measure M2 as the baseline, and update the experiments document with the refined M3 model. Group: build the M2-vs-M3 comparison table and cross-validate against the defined parameters. The open gate is the harness wrapper (sweeps + metrics + aggregation) and a deterministic core over the pure state; the current spike is still tokio/wall-clock.

## [Analysis of existing pubsub protocols](https://github.com/input-output-hk/pubsub/issues/65)

The network model consolidated on **M3**, now the primary framework for analysis, to be validated against experimental data. M3 removes golden nodes as initial seeds and instead uses hashes to control fan-out and seeding, targeting global graph properties; initial results show a high probability of forming a "good graph" for a 20,000-node network, with bad-graph formations expected to be rare. The team agreed to **exclude golden nodes from the initial analytical model** — their contribution dilutes as the network grows (they lower the Request Factor by only one), so they are not critical for long-term scalability accuracy. Terminology sharpened around **publishing links** vs **relaying (downstream) links**: in M3, S-links (seed links) exist purely to initiate dissemination when a node has no upstream, avoiding an artificial Request Factor bump, and are established hash-based like upstream links. Denis and Ezequiel noted M2 bidirectional and M2 undirected are effectively the same.

The **SecureCyclon** three-layer gossip stack was formally retired as the settled external position, in favour of the simpler on-chain-anchored approach. In parallel, Denis (with Sandro) is building formulas for three properties to compare protocol models, aiming at a generic, abstractable system for cross-validation against prior findings.

Next: complete the per-model comparison formulas and cross-validate them against the simulation results as the harness produces data.

## [Product Requirements & Use Case Definition](https://github.com/input-output-hk/pubsub/issues/11)

**Blink Labs joined the weekly sessions.** Chris Gianelloni presented **Raven**, their secure messenger on the Cardano node (DMQ, SIP 137, Signal protocol), currently SPO-driven over a trusted topology, with willingness to adapt toward PubSub's discoverable topic-based architecture to avoid duplicated effort. Blink also brings **Adder**, a lightweight Go ETL/notification pipeline (chain-sync/mempool in; Discord, Telegram, webhook out) that doubles as an embeddable library for wallet notifications. The initial collaboration anchors on the **SPO-notification use case** — adding a PubSub input to Adder — slotting into the four standing use cases (emergency SPO alerts, SPO-to-delegator, dApp-to-user, and governance communication).

On economics, the current model tolerates a ~20% adversarial fraction but explicitly does not cover resource exhaustion / DoS; the iteration's posture is empirical evidence for the security bounds plus honest gap identification (incentives, fees, slashing are future research). Resource analysis is being folded into M3 against Sybil attacks by bounding accepted connections (S and RF), with parameter exploration showing higher S/RF forces attackers to acquire more identities. Registration-deposit shape (fixed vs network-size-relative) remains open.

Next: continue the Adder integration with Blink Labs; keep deposit/attack-budget sizing analytical (tolerable adversarial fraction and identity cost) rather than fixing a dollar figure now.
