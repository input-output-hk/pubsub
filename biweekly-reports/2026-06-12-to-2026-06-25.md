# PubSub - Bi-Weekly Report: 12 June - 25 June 2026

## Executive Summary

This period split between hardening the prototype and a significant correction to the security analysis. On the implementation side, the node gained real connection management, message publishing with fan-out, a cross-registry consistency invariant, and pluggable strategy seams for connection selection, connection acceptance, and fan-out — six features/refactors merged behind path-filtered CI. On the research side, the central finding was that **SecureCyclon is not Cyclon-with-defences**: its non-swappable descriptors change the protocol's internal dynamics, so the earlier Cyclon analysis does not transfer. A targeted-eclipsing simulator quantified the residual *view-violation* risk (≈15% view bias at 5% adversarial nodes, full eclipse at 20%), with view commitments proposed as the candidate mitigation. The two weekly/brainstorm sessions then made a strategic call: **drop the dedicated peer-sampling layer** in favour of the on-chain list plus rejection sampling — redirecting effort toward an experimentation framework and golden-node modelling. The main open question is whether view commitments are feasible and worth their cost ahead of the 6 July phase boundary.

## [Rust PubSub node prototype](https://github.com/input-output-hk/pubsub/issues/36)

The prototype moved from a read-only state core to a connected, message-passing node with switchable behaviour. **Connection management** ([PR #56](https://github.com/input-output-hk/pubsub/pull/56), [`004-connections`](../pubsub-node/specs/004-connections/), ADRs [0017](../pubsub-node/docs/decisions/0017-key-backed-peer-identity-and-signed-connection-control.md)/[0019](../pubsub-node/docs/decisions/0019-graceful-shutdown-lifecycle.md)) introduced a key-backed `PeerId`, signed connection-control messages, an autonomous static topology with handshake establishment, silent-misbehaviour severance, and graceful shutdown. **Message publishing + fan-out** ([PR #67](https://github.com/input-output-hk/pubsub/pull/67), [`006-fanout-policy`](../pubsub-node/specs/006-fanout-policy/), ADR [0021](../pubsub-node/docs/decisions/0021-fanout-strategy-seam-dedup-and-message-origin.md)) added the publish path, first-hop fan-out, onward relay through the mesh, and forwarding-loop suppression behind a fan-out strategy seam with message-origin tracking and dedup. **Cross-registry consistency** ([PR #63](https://github.com/input-output-hk/pubsub/pull/63), [`014-registry-consistency`](../pubsub-node/specs/014-registry-consistency/), ADR [0020](../pubsub-node/docs/decisions/0020-cross-registry-consistency-and-readiness.md)) collapsed readiness onto a single registry indexer with one dial trigger and a consistency invariant between the subscription list and topic registry.

This realises the three pluggable strategy interfaces framed in the 16 June session: connection selection ([ADR 0018](../pubsub-node/docs/decisions/0018-connection-selection-strategy-seam.md)) and connection acceptance ([ADR 0023](../pubsub-node/docs/decisions/0023-connection-acceptance-strategy-seam.md), [PR #68](https://github.com/input-output-hk/pubsub/pull/68)) are now extracted seams, alongside the fan-out seam — so churn and adversarial scenarios can be configured per node without touching the core. A maintenance pass ([issue #71](https://github.com/input-output-hk/pubsub/issues/71)) renamed `Message::Signed → Message::Dissemination`, harmonised strategy field/param names, and retired settle-sleeps from the async tests per ADR [0022](../pubsub-node/docs/decisions/0022-async-test-synchronization-strategy.md) ([PR #70](https://github.com/input-output-hk/pubsub/pull/70)).

Next: build the experimentation framework on top of these seams (message-propagation paths, hop counts, configurable scenarios, metrics) and model golden nodes via connection-acceptance and discovery policies; quantify attack budgets against deposit values.

## [Empirical analysis of silent attacks on SecureCyclon](https://github.com/input-output-hk/pubsub/issues/72)

The headline research correction: the team's working assumption that SecureCyclon behaves like Cyclon with added defences is **incorrect** ([issue #43](https://github.com/input-output-hk/pubsub/issues/43)). SecureCyclon forbids certain descriptor reuse to block silent cloning, and those non-swappable descriptors alter the protocol's internal dynamics — so the prior Cyclon uniformity/attack analysis holds for Cyclon but does *not* transfer to SecureCyclon. Treating it as standard Cyclon would mask real vulnerabilities.

A targeted-eclipsing simulator built in coordination with Spyros quantified the residual risk. Simple silent attacks are mitigated by the descriptor rules, but **view-violation** (concentration-based) attacks remain significant: an adversary can drive ≈15% view bias with 5% of the network adversarial, reaching a full node eclipse at 20%. **Commitments on the view** were proposed as the candidate defence to neutralise these attacks.

Next: evaluate the feasibility and cost of view commitments; keep the SecureCyclon analysis current ahead of the 6 July phase boundary.

## [Analysis of existing pubsub protocols](https://github.com/input-output-hk/pubsub/issues/65)

The related-work survey was structured against the agreed inclusion criteria (Byzantine-resilient, broker-free), shortlisting five candidates for closer analysis ([RPS candidate discovery, issue #64](https://github.com/input-output-hk/pubsub/issues/64)). Fireflies was noted for its clever node arrangement but flagged for linear scaling, which makes it impractical beyond hundreds of thousands of nodes. The growing complexity of SecureCyclon was weighed against simpler protocols such as Basalt that present a smaller attack surface, and the team agreed to fold a *practical sanity check* into the analysis — e.g. for Cardano SPOs, a "leave and rejoin on detecting an issue" response may beat over-engineering Byzantine resistance — to avoid unnecessary complexity.

Next: a final report per protocol ahead of the 6 July phase boundary.

## [Peer sampling dropped — list-based with rejection sampling](https://github.com/input-output-hk/pubsub/issues/35)

The 25 June brainstorm closed out the dedicated peer-sampling thread. Since the design already carries an on-chain registry of deposited participants, a separate sampling protocol adds attack surface and reduces overall security for little gain. Offline nodes are treated as a bounded form of misbehaviour (covered by the existing tolerance formulas) rather than a first-class feature, and a node that draws an offline peer simply **re-samples** — rejection sampling over the on-chain list replaces the sampling layer. Online/offline status is surfaced via IP discovery and gossip heartbeats. Registry scale is bounded by transaction throughput rather than storage, with data-availability layers or indexers (e.g. DB Sync) serving peer-status queries.

A dissemination-layer flooding surface was identified — a node requesting to be the downstream of unbounded participants — with sequence numbers, hash/VRF bucketing, and deposit tuning as candidate mitigations. On punishment, proving non-delivery is hard, but observable misbehaviour (exceeding agreed connection parameters) can be signed and proven, enabling slashing or reputation loss.

Next: Will and the team to define which experiments to explore with the prototype, with Ezequiel building the framework to describe, configure, and run them plus golden-node configuration; Denis to finalise the gossip and broadcast documentation, removing peer sampling from the active roadmap.

## [Product requirements and incentive direction](https://github.com/input-output-hk/pubsub/issues/11)

The weekly session set the architectural envelope around the product. **Light/edge clients are excluded from the protocol topology** — mobile apps and wallet providers act as proxies for push notifications instead. **Sybil resistance** rests on a registration cost framed as a deposit/collateral (not staking); all participants must register keys regardless of the mechanism chosen, though reusing existing certificates may be more efficient for specific entities. **Incentives** are anchored on dApps and B2B/B2C use cases (e.g. embedded intents for mobile/web apps) as the primary driver, with application revenue potentially subsidising governance and emergency-alert channels rather than those carrying the model alone. Persistence/data-availability requirements are pushed to the application layer rather than baked into the core protocol.

Next: continue developing the application-revenue use cases; fold deposit/penalty sizing into the incentive model.
