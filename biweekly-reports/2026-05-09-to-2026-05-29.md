## [Three-layer stack falsified; list-based architecture adopted](https://github.com/input-output-hk/pubsub/issues/9)

The team aligned on a list-based replacement for the inherited SecureCyclon + Vicinity + hybrid-dissemination stack. Peer sampling and navigation collapse into a single on-chain subscription list (one entry per node, carrying its topic-interest set); the dissemination layer is unchanged; module interfaces are held fixed so a future multi-peer-sampling protocol delivered by research can be slotted in without re-architecture. The decision and the architecture rationale are captured in [`docs/technical-report-1.md`](../docs/technical-report-1.md) and the operational flows in [`docs/list-based-architecture.md`](../docs/list-based-architecture.md). ## [Empirical analysis of Cyclon uniformity (D1.3 falsified)](https://github.com/input-output-hk/pubsub/issues/41)

Cyclon's three uniformity properties were tested empirically. D1.1 (marginal) and D1.2 (per-view) hold; **D1.3 (overlay-graph uniformity) is falsified** under the deterministic per-cycle initiation rule Cyclon specifies. Total-variation distance from a uniform random `c`-out digraph grows monotonically with N and exceeds 0.48 at N=10⁴. The bias is structural, not a finite-size artifact: deterministic self-injection combined with in-degree-proportional partnering concentrates the in-degree distribution tighter than uniform. Critically, D1.3 is the property that licenses *sample-from-view ≡ sample-from-network* in all higher-layer eclipse and partition arguments — its falsification undermines every security bound stacked on top of Cyclon. Details: [`cyclon_properties_report.md`](../formal_spec/peer_sampling/cyclon/cyclon_properties_report.md). The Poisson-initiation fix restores marginal uniformity but breaks SecureCyclon's frequency-check defence against hub attacks.

Next: issue closed. Result feeds the architecture and silent-attack workstreams.

## [Silent attack analysis on Cyclon and SecureCyclon](https://github.com/input-output-hk/pubsub/issues/43)

A second formal-analysis pass identified a silent view-bias attack against Cyclon-family protocols. An adversary running the protocol externally with full fidelity — but privately dropping honest entries from its own view and prioritising adversary-pointing entries when sampling the gossip subset — can distort the global view distribution without committing any protocol violation. None of SecureCyclon's nine deterministic defences fire. At a 10% adversary fraction the attack achieves a 3.9× amplification (honest views become ~40% adversary-pointing); at 15% the figure saturates at 93%. The attack reaches equilibrium within ~60 cycles and persists indefinitely with no natural decay. Reproducible simulator and full write-up: [`cyclon_silent_bias_attack.md`](../formal_spec/peer_sampling/cyclon/cyclon_silent_bias_attack.md). The result is cited directly in the technical report as part of the case against the three-layer stack.

Next: investigate mitigation strategies (view commitments and slowing adversarial speed to protocol frequency) for the joint research thread with Spyros; surface link-dropping as the dominant silent-attack vector for the dissemination-layer analysis.

## [Brainstorm with Spyros: SecureCyclon verdict and joint research direction](https://github.com/input-output-hk/pubsub/issues/35)

Spyros Voulgaris reviewed the technical report and joined a 28 May session with Will and Denis. Outcome: SecureCyclon is judged unviable for high-adversary, financially-incentivised environments like blockchain — published threat models are unrealistic for these settings. Three substantive directions came out of the session ([logbook 2026-05-28](../logbook.md#2026-05-28--brainstorm-with-spyros-securecyclon-verdict-and-the-path-forward)):

1. **Dynamic ring positions via epoch nonce.** Derive each node's ring position from the hash of its public key combined with a per-epoch randomness source (e.g. Cardano epoch nonce), with at least one epoch of delay between descriptor commit and use. Closes the position-grinding surface in Vicinity.
2. **Fan-out 4–5 as the practical setting.** The current architecture document's fan-out of 2 is a floor; production should run 4–5 to improve robustness without exploding overhead.
3. **Link dropping is the dominant silent attack.** Biased views can be detected and bounded; dropped links degrade delivery directly. View commitments and bounding adversarial speed identified as candidate mitigations.

A joint research publication on secure peer-sampling layers was agreed as a follow-on collaboration. Spyros will simulate the bias-view behaviour against the reported malicious-node percentages and review the scaffolding once his teaching obligations end.

Next: add Spyros as reviewer on the architecture PR; develop a testable formula sufficient to establish dissemination-layer security; Denis to continue analysing mitigation strategies ahead of the next session.
